About

      
I am a PhD candidate in Experimental Medicine at McGill University, in the D3MOD lab. My research interests are Bayesian methods, sequential Monte Carlo and machine learning for infectious disease epidemiology.

      
I obtained my MBBS from the Faculty of Medicine, University of Peradeniya, and worked as a medical doctor in both the curative and preventive health sectors. I then joined the University of Peradeniya as a lecturer, before coming to McGill for my PhD.

      
Outside research, I photograph the night sky: nebulae, galaxies, comets and the Moon.

Research

      
Epidemic modelling and surveillance
Adaptive sampling / Particle filters / State-space models for infectious disease
State-space models of infectious disease, fitted with particle filters as surveillance data arrive, and adaptive sampling that uses the model's forecast to set the size of the next round. Earlier work on transmission estimates and outbreak simulation.
Bayesian methods
Sequential Monte Carlo / Bayesian assurance / Bayesian meta-analysis
Sequential Monte Carlo for models that are refitted as data accumulate, and Bayesian assurance for choosing sample sizes, both before a study and round by round during one. Bayesian meta-analysis to pool published clinical evidence.

      
This is a live stochastic SIR epidemic on a 120 x 120 grid: each day an infected person infects each neighbour with probability 0.1, rarely someone far away, and recovers with probability 0.15. Tap the grid to start an epidemic there. SIR model code

Current work
A model-based adaptive sampling framework for infectious disease surveillance
S Manathunga, N Osgood, SF O'Brien, DL Buckeridge, WA Russell
arXiv:2611.01199, 2026
*Lee B. Lusted Student Prize, SMDM 2026
Quantitative Methods and Theoretical Developments, for the poster "Using a stochastic epidemic model to guide efficient seroprevalence sampling decisions".
How many samples does the next surveillance round really need? A particle filter keeps a state-space epidemic model up to date as data arrive. Its one-step-ahead prediction feeds a Bayesian assurance criterion, which picks the cheapest design likely to reach a target credible-interval width. Tested in simulation and on SARS-CoV-2 serosurveillance of Canadian blood donors.
